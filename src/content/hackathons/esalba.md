## What we built

**eSalba** is an offline-first emergency continuity and triage system. When a disaster knocks out mobile data, emergency reports still need to reach the LGU. eSalba is built for exactly that window: capture the report locally, triage it, and sync the moment any connection comes back.

It grew out of what we learned building [SALBAR](/hackathons/salbar) in Naga, where the problem was the same but the audience was citizens instead of local government.

## The build

With roughly **12 hours** of actual building time, we turned the idea into a working prototype and an 8-minute pitch video.

Huge shoutout to **Edward**, **Eduardo**, **Abby**, and **Isabella**. I genuinely couldn't have asked for a better team.

## The result

We won. The awarding happened in September, months after the build, with a **₱100,000** grant for the finalist teams.

## Honest notes

The event itself had serious problems. The judging criteria were never made clear, the process lacked transparency, and so much time went to waiting that a multi-day hackathon barely felt like one.

Teams deserve to know how their work is being evaluated, especially when they spend the night actually building it.

I'm grateful we won. I also hope the next one is better. Both can be true.
